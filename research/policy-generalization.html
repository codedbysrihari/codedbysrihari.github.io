Reinforcement learning

Beyond the Training Board

A proposed extension of my PPO Snake project that tests policies on unseen layouts. It would compare training strategies using fixed interaction budgets and expose failure cases through replayable episodes and clearly separated evaluation maps.

Research question

Does a Snake agent learn a transferable strategy or depend on familiar environments?

Why explore this

My existing Snake project provides a starting point for studying policy generalization. A strong score on familiar boards leaves an open question about behaviour when obstacles and routes change. Research on procedurally generated environments motivates explicit training and test separation. This proposal would turn that question into a controlled evaluation with inspectable failures and reproducible episode seeds.

Working hypothesis

Training across varied layouts may improve performance on unseen maps within a fixed interaction budget. A staged curriculum may change that benefit. The experiment would measure both transfer and the cost in learning speed on familiar layouts.

Experiment design

  1. Define a fixed 12-by-12 Snake environment with a consistent observation and action space. Implement seeded obstacle layouts and food placement. Validate reachability at reset and document rewards, termination conditions, and the maximum episode length.
  2. Separate layout seeds into training, validation, and final-test partitions. Hold out complete layout families, such as narrow corridors, for an additional structural-shift test. Hash generated maps to detect accidental overlap.
  3. Compare three PPO training conditions using one architecture and reward function. Train on a fixed layout set, randomized layouts, and a staged curriculum over randomized layouts. Match total environment transitions and model-selection budgets.
  4. Use five independent training seeds per condition. Select checkpoints through validation episodes using a predeclared rule. Include a legal-action random policy and a shortest-path-to-food heuristic as interpretable reference points.
  5. Freeze all policies before evaluating 100 episode seeds per test family. Pair map and episode seeds across methods. Save observations, actions, rewards, and terminal causes so a React replay can show where a policy succeeds or fails.

Evaluation plan

  • Report mean apples collected, episode length, and collision or starvation rates for each held-out family. Include the full score distribution and counts of evaluated episodes.
  • Measure the gap between familiar-layout and unseen-layout performance. Keep structural shifts visible as separate results so an aggregate score cannot hide a particular failure mode.
  • Plot learning curves against environment transitions and wall time. Compare transfer at matched interaction budgets and record the compute required by each training condition.
  • Report every training-seed score and bootstrap intervals that resample training seeds, with episodes nested within each seed. Treat uncertainty cautiously with five runs and describe exploratory findings as such.
  • Perform a curriculum-order ablation with identical map exposure counts, difficulty mixtures, and total transition budgets, changing only presentation order. Use replay analysis to classify recurring failures, including loops, obstacle traps, and inefficient routes.

Limits and risks

  • A small grid environment offers controlled experiments with a restricted state and action space. Transfer to robotics or other physical tasks would require additional evidence.
  • Reachable initial maps can still produce difficult or trapping states during play. The environment report would distinguish generator defects from policy failures.
  • Several episodes from one trained policy share a common training history. The evaluation would preserve that grouping when estimating uncertainty.

Implementation plan

  1. Audit the existing agent and specify the environment contract and split manifests.
  2. Validate map generation, references, and deterministic episode replay.
  3. Run the matched-budget comparison and curriculum ablation.
  4. Publish a report and a replay gallery linked to complete experiment records.

Planned outputs

  • A versioned generalization benchmark for the Snake environment.
  • A report covering training efficiency, held-out performance, and uncertainty.
  • A replay interface that makes policy decisions and failure cases inspectable.
